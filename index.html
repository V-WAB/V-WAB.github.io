The story

My mother never called it skincare.

She just made sure our skin was deeply nourished, using pure shea butter on my siblings and me every single day. It was simply what we knew.

The shea I grew up with was magic, but it demanded patience, especially with my busy schedule. Wrestling with a hard block on a rushed morning, dealing with the heavy grease it left on clothes, and for some, the strong smell, simply became a struggle.

I realised something then: the gap was never the butter. It was everything around the butter, the consistency, the packaging, the everyday experience, and Banini is me giving that shea butter the brand it always deserved.

It is not an attempt to invent something new. It is a return to that original care, held to a standard that travels.

Through Banini Butter, I am honouring the soul of the shea while making it an absolute dream to use by making it light and effortless on your skin for all skin types.

I am taking this beautiful, homegrown ingredient from Ghana and proving it belongs front and centre on the highest shelves, proudly showing the world that our native ingredients are the ultimate luxury and that we can have both the rich authenticity of our roots and a beautiful, effortless experience without pretending, even slightly, to be from anywhere else but Ghana.

Nurtured by Nature, from her hands to yours.Founder, Banini Butter, Accra

The jar

Four things in, nothing else.

Every jar is whipped, never melted and poured. Melting collapses the crystal structure and gives you the greasy film most people think shea has to leave. Whipping keeps it light, so it sinks in and stays put.

  • Base

    Unrefined shea butterGathered by women's cooperatives near Tamale, in the Northern Region.

  • Oil

    Cold-pressed baobab oilPressed without heat, so the vitamins survive the journey into the jar.

  • Method

    Whipped, not meltedHand-churned in small batches until the texture holds a soft peak.

  • Never

    No parabens, no fillers, no waterNothing added to make the jar heavier or the margin easier.

Where we stand

The shelf we intend to sit on.

Two questions decide this category. Does it come from where it says it comes from, and does it feel worth the money. Most shea sits low on one axis or the other. We are building for the corner where both hold.

Premium and
authentically African

Imported feel ↔ Authentically African Mass-market ↔ Premium

  • Banini Butter

    Whipped by hand in Accra from nuts we can trace to the women who gathered them in the north, finished to a standard that holds its own in any beauty hall. Ghanaian on the label and in the jar.

  • Naya by Africa

    Our closest local rival and proof the demand is real. Strongly African in its story, though the finish and the price still read closer to everyday care than to luxury.

  • L'Occitane

    The global benchmark for what shea can command. It buys Burkinabé butter, then sells it as French. That gap is the opening we walk through.

  • Market-stall raw butter

    The most honest shea in Ghana and the least consistent. Beautiful ingredient, no packaging, no testing, no reason for anyone abroad to trust it.

  • Supermarket own-brand

    Shea listed fourth on an ingredient panel, cut with water and filler. Cheap, widely stocked, and forgettable.
